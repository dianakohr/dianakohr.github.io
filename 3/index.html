A.3: Warp the Images

In order of: original, nearest neighbors, bilinear



Bilinear is slightly better than nearest neighbors in producing a smooth, rectified image. For example, nearest neighbors causes you to lose a chunk of the character k in the example with ducks. In addition, in the photos of paintings, nearest neighbors distorts the text on the placards. For me, bilinear was slower, but the overall runtime difference was negligible.

A.4: Blend the Images into a Mosaic

The 2 leftmost images are the original photos. Using manually chosen correspondences to compute the homography, we warp the second image into the third image. The last 3 images are various attempts to stitch the pieces of the mosaic together. The first attempt is a simple overlay, followed by a weighted average, which both produce sharp edges. The last one is the product of Laplacian blending (project 2)!

In order of: originals, warped image, blended mosaic

Naive Harris corner detection on the left (I chose min_distance=5 in peak_local_max to thin out the detected corners), with Adaptive Non-Maximal Suppression on the right. For ANMS, I followed the paper's threshold of 0.9, which worked well.

In order of: manual mosaic, autostitched


Overall, 4-point RANSAC was able to match the images quite well! The only obvious visible difference is in the bay mosaic (top right), where the computed homography led to a small, slightly blurry wedge above the sun.
